## Research Summary

We can actually fully demystify my thesis title, “Dynamics of matter wave quantum emitters in engineered reservoirs”, as we have already done the hard work. Dynamics is just a physics-y way of saying “how things move”, so my thesis work was repeatedly preparing high energy two-state systems with atoms in them, shaping an environment (using lasers!) and then letting the system evolve some time before measuring the results. 

## Ok, Mike, that’s great, but you haven’t told me “how” or “why”

Yes, my astute reader, I haven’t really covered the “why” or the “how”. Let me handle the “why” first and later I can give a flavor of the “how”. 

Why do this research at all? Because it’s interesting! End of discussion! 

Just kidding of course. It is interesting in its own right, but let’s get practical. I mentioned quantum computing. One thing that classical computers (like the phone you’re probably reading this on) are great at is storing data. Classical computers memory is essentially a series of zeros and ones stored in a magnetic tape somewhere in your machine (to oversimplify), and computer programming is the art of taking those zeros and ones and converting them into usable outputs, like this Next.js website. Classical memories are really good in the sense that if I write some zeros and ones, and then come back days, or even years, later, I can recover the sequence of zeros and ones that I set way back in the past. 

Quantum mechanics, as it usually does, makes things messier. I mentioned that a quantum bit (qubit) can be a zero, a one, or some combination thereof. This is a really powerful fact for certain computing applications (come back for my series on elliptic curves and cryptography, if you’re a masochist), but it comes with a drawback. Qubits are very sensitive. If I set some qubits into zeros, ones, or superpositions, and come back a short time later, I will likely only read out zeros. This decay happens pretty quickly! Even one second, while being nothing to us, is a long time for a quantum system. 

Enter my research! In a way, the noisy environment around a qubit that causes it to decay is just a reservoir. By studying how the connection between two-state systems and reservoirs, as well as reservoir shapes, affect the decay of the initially excited states, we can learn something about how to prevent quantum decays for longer times. As my research shows, with sufficient care in designing the environment, we can even achieve partial or full revivals of lost quantum memory at well defined times! Hopefully this gives an idea as to why we might be interested in this sort of research project.
