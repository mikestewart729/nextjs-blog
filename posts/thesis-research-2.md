## Quantum Emitters and Engineered Reservoirs

Having discussed quantum mechanics broadly, let’s focus on a certain class of quantum systems. Suppose we have a quantum system which can access only two energy states, one with high energy (excited) and one with low energy (ground). Such systems exist in nature and are of general interest, for example, in quantum computing. Quantum bits are like classical bits in your computer in that they can be set to 0 or 1, but in the quantum case, they can also be set to a _superposition_ of 0 and 1, which leads to powerful new ways to compute. 

Anyway, suppose we have one of these two-state systems. Furthermore, let’s suppose we connect this system to a large number of other quantum states having various energies. We can call this large set of states a reservoir or bath. If we use laboratory techniques, we can shape the reservoir to look a certain way. By shaping the reservoir, we can change how easily our two state system can connect to the reservoir’s states. Once the two are connected, we can let the system evolve and observe how the shape of the reservoir changes the behavior of the two-state system.

Let’s take a breath and de-jargonize (I’m truly at a loss for what the correct word is here, but by now I know you’ll forgive me; you’re locked in for the physics!) more of my thesis title. “Matter wave quantum emitters in engineered reservoirs” is simply a physicist’s way of saying we have two-state systems of matter, which we start in their high energy state. These “quantum emitters” will tend to, by a connection to a reservoir, emit their contained atom, which we know behaves like a matter wave, and end up in their low energy state. These emitted matter waves then travel along through our reservoirs, which we can shape or change using laboratory techniques. By changing the shape, we can change the physics!
